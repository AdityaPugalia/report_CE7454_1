\begin{abstract}
This project addresses AI-generated image detection: each $512\times512$ JPEG must be attributed to a real camera or to one of two undisclosed generators, using a single model of at most 12.3M parameters trained from scratch. Image generators leave a faint repeating pattern in their images as a side effect of how they up-sample. A ResNet-18 baseline that is given this pattern explicitly, through the neighbouring-pixel residual, scores 100\% on validation and dev data but labels every test image as real. The test images had been post-processed, which shifts the generators' pattern, and the baseline only recognises the pattern in one fixed position. Our model keeps the residual input, replaces the baseline's strided down-sampling with anti-aliased BlurPool layers, and is trained on random crops of broadly augmented copies of the training images. It scores \textbf{96.33\%} on test and 96.1\% on dev, against 33.3\% on test for the baseline. Tests on validation images show that it is largely insensitive to shifts, flips and compression, and almost all of its remaining errors are real photographs labelled as fake.
\end{abstract}
