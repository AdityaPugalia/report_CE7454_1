\begin{abstract}
We attribute $512\times512$ JPEGs to a camera or to one of two undisclosed generators with a single from-scratch model of at most 12.3M parameters. A ResNet-18 that reads the image and its neighbouring-pixel residual at full resolution scores 100\% on held-out and development data, yet labels all 1,500 test images as real. The test images were post-processed, which moves the generators' period-8 up-sampling pattern relative to the pixel grid, and the detector only recognises the pattern at a fixed position. Our submitted model keeps the residual input but down-samples with anti-aliasing BlurPool layers. It is trained on $256^2$ crops of 152k stored, broadly augmented copies and applied once to each test image as given. It scores \textbf{95.3\%} on test (the baseline's all-real output scores 33.3\%) and 96.1\% on development data. Probes show that our model is invariant to sub-period shifts and flips. Almost all remaining errors are real photographs labelled as fake.
\end{abstract}
