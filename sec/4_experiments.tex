\subsection{Main result}
The baseline is perfect on clean and dev data but labels every test image as real (\cref{tab:main}). My model gives up 0.3 points of clean accuracy and scores 96.1\% on dev and \textbf{96.33\%} on test and I ranked \textbf{41} on the leaderboard (\textbf{Top 30\%})

\subsection{What does my model read differently?}
\Cref{fig:probes} shows the perturbations applied to the validation images without retraining.
The baseline drops to chance for every shift in the grid phase that is not a multiple of 8 and for flips and rotations. These move or mirror the grid without removing it, which confirms the diagnosis [~\Cref{sec:diagnosis}]. My model is unaffected (99.7\%).
\emph{Scale.} A $\pm10\%$ rescale is fatal for the baseline (33\%) and costs my model 8--12 points. Halving the resolution, outside the training range, leaves my model at 80.9\%.
\emph{Compression and noise.} JPEG q50 costs my model 2 points (baseline: 53), and Gaussian noise with $\sigma{=}5$ costs it 3.5 points (baseline: 36). The one cost of robustness is blur: $\sigma{=}1$ costs my model 2.3 points and the baseline only 0.1.

\subsection{Ablations and training behaviour}
\noindent\textbf{Crop size and class rebalancing.} A variant trained with $128^2$ crops and 20 copies of the smoothest 25\% of real images ties my model (dev 96.0\%). Its best-dev checkpoint reaches 97.3\% (epoch 20), but on 900 dev images a 1-point difference is within noise, so I select on harsh validation rather than dev.

\noindent\textbf{Invariance is slow to learn.} The baseline passes 99.9\% after two epochs of 12k images. My model needs 21 epochs of 152k crops to pass 98\% on harsh validation (\cref{fig:curves}),thus, invariance must be learnt from many shifted and rescaled examples. 

\subsection{Error analysis}
Almost all of my model's dev errors are real images called fake, and Models~A and~B are never confused with each other. Real recall rises with texture, from 84\% for the smoothest quarter of real images to 92--93\% for the two most textured, which suggests my model partly relies on the scene statistics of the training reals.
